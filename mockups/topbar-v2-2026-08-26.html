The bar edge, three ways

The bar and the page are the same blue, so the bar has no bottom and the two run together. Three ways out, drawn on the group page you are looking at now.

One · White bar, blue page

The bar turns white, the tabs go brand blue, and the open tab becomes the pale panel we already use on cards. The edge is absolute, the mark sits on its own ground rather than in a white box, and the heaviest colour on the screen stops being the chrome.

What it costs. The tabs lose the white-on-blue contrast they have now, so the open tab has to work harder. What it buys. Nothing else on the page has to change.

Two · Blue bar, and the blue stops there

The bar keeps its blue and becomes the only blue. The page underneath turns to the near-white we already use behind cards, and the cards take a light border so they still read as cards. This is the strongest hierarchy of the three, and the biggest change to the pages themselves.

Three · Blue bar, and the page lifts under it

Nothing changes colour. The bar gets a real bottom edge and a soft shadow, and the page content rises into a rounded sheet that tucks under it. The bleed goes away because the page now has a top, not because the blue went away.

What it costs. A strip of blue stays visible above the sheet, which is either a frame or a leftover depending on how you read it. What it buys. It is the only one of the three that is purely a change to the shared file, so it lands everywhere at once and nothing else moves.

What I would pick

One, the white bar. Our whole visual language is white cards floating on blue, and a white bar joins that language instead of arguing with it. The chrome stops being the loudest thing on the screen, the edge is unmistakable, and every page keeps working exactly as it does today.

Two is the right answer eventually, and it is a bigger job than the bar. When the pages move off Glide properly, the blue body is the next thing to go.

The marks in the My events rail

The mark takes the place the number holds today, and the numbers move to a line under the rail where there is room to say what they are. The tab says what the section is, the mark says how much you have done, and the line says what is ahead.

What changed from your rail today. "Attending (1)" becomes "Attending" with a mark where the number was. The number moves under the rail and says what it means, so "1 coming up" instead of a bare 1 that reads like a score.

Why the marks differ per row. Each section counts its own lifetime total, so Socialized at 115 already holds the full mark while Attending at 12 holds the second step.

On the open tab the mark goes white, because the tab it sits on is solid blue. Everywhere else it keeps its orange.

Numbers that fall never earn a mark. The rail counts what is coming up, and that number drops as events pass. The marks count what you have already done, so a mark can never take itself back.
